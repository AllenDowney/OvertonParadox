## LinkedIn

"If you are not a liberal at 25, you have no heart. If you are not a conservative at 35, you have no brain."

Is that true? Do people get more conservative as they get older?

In Probably Overthinking It, I used data from the General Social Survey to show that the quip is half right. Older people are more conservative — but as people get older, they don't become more conservative. On average, they become a little more liberal.

Now I've updated that analysis with data through 2024 and better methods. The conclusion holds:

• In every decade of the survey, older people give more conservative answers than younger people.

• But following people born in the same year, their answers get a little more liberal over time — about 0.8 percentage points per decade.

• Since 1972, average conservatism has fallen by about 16 points. More than two-thirds of that is generational replacement; the rest is people changing their minds.

The figure shows one line for each birth year from 1900 to 2006. Each one starts lower than the one before, and every one drifts down.

All of this depends on which questions count as "conservative." If you think I chose badly, you can choose your own and run the whole analysis in your browser: https://allendowney.github.io/OvertonParadox/overtron/
